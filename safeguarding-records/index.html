Support careful professional decision-making

The BACP Ethical Framework places client wellbeing, confidentiality and protection from serious harm at the centre of practice. In exceptional circumstances, safeguarding a client or another person from serious harm may require information to be disclosed despite a client’s wishes.

PracticeKit helps document the process around a concern. It does not assess risk, recommend an intervention or replace consultation with a safeguarding lead, supervisor, employer or relevant authority.

Concern details

Record the date and a factual account of what was observed, said or disclosed.

Fact and interpretation

Use structured notes to distinguish direct information from professional opinion or interpretation.

People informed

Record who was consulted or informed, when contact occurred and the purpose of the communication.

Actions and follow-up

Document decisions, actions taken, agreed next steps and subsequent developments.

Confidentiality has foreseeable limits

Clients should be informed in advance about foreseeable limits to confidentiality, including safeguarding responsibilities and disclosures required or authorised by law. When information is shared, the practitioner should consider what is necessary, proportionate and consistent with applicable law, policy and the client agreement.

Safeguarding responsibilities vary by client group, setting, nation, law and organisational policy. Always follow the procedures and escalation routes that apply to your work.

Maintain a useful chronology

A concern may develop across several contacts. Keeping disclosures, observations, consultations, decisions and actions in a chronological history can support continuity, supervision, accountability and any later review.

Records should remain factual, adequate and relevant. Avoid speculation, unnecessary sensitive detail or language that obscures whether something was witnessed, reported by another person or professionally inferred.
